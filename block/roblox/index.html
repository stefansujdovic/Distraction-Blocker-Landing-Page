How to block Roblox on PC

Updated November 3, 2026

The quickest way: add Roblox to Distraction Blocker's app list and roblox.com to its website list, then start a focus session. Roblox won't open — in the app or any browser — until the break, and it can't be switched off without restarting the PC. It's free and needs no account. Roblox's own parental controls and Microsoft Family Safety are covered below too.

Method 1 · about 2 minutes

Block Roblox with Distraction Blocker

  1. Install it on your child's PC. Download the free installer and run it. Windows may warn about an unknown publisher — here's why that's fine.
  2. Add Roblox to the blocked apps. This covers the Roblox Player that games launch in.
  3. Add roblox.com to the blocked websites. This stops browsing and launching games from the Roblox website in every browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, any of them.
  4. Set homework time and press Start. For example 25-minute study blocks with 5-minute breaks. Roblox stays blocked during each study block and unlocks in the breaks.

Once a session is running there's no pause button, no password to guess and no uninstall. The only way out is restarting the PC — which you'll notice.

Method 2: Roblox's built-in parental controls

Roblox lets a parent link their own Roblox account to their child's and manage it from there. Once linked, you can set a daily playtime limit, limit who can chat with your child, filter content by age, and cap Robux spending. Because the limits are attached to the account, they apply on every device — PC, phone, tablet and Xbox.

  • Sign in to your parent Roblox account and open Settings → Parental Controls.
  • Link your child's account (Roblox walks you through it).
  • Set the daily time limit and other restrictions.

Good to know: a daily limit decides how much Roblox your child plays, not when. An hour of Roblox right after school still uses up homework time. Roblox changes these menus from time to time, so check the current labels in its help center.

Method 3: Microsoft Family Safety

If your child signs in to Windows with their own Microsoft account in your family group, you can manage apps and games from family.microsoft.com or the Family Safety app:

  • Choose your child, then Windows → Apps and games.
  • Find Roblox and either block it or set a daily time limit and allowed hours.
  • Add roblox.com to blocked websites under content filters (this applies in Microsoft Edge).

Good to know: Family Safety only works when your child uses their own Microsoft account on the PC — not a shared or local account — and its website filters only apply in Edge, so it's common to block other browsers too.

Which one should you use?

  • Roblox should wait until homework is done → Distraction Blocker. Start a session when homework starts; Roblox comes back in the breaks and afterwards.
  • You want a daily cap everywhere → Roblox parental controls, because they follow the account to every device.
  • You want to manage the whole PC and your child already has a Microsoft account → Family Safety.
  • Both → Use Roblox's daily limit for the overall amount, and Distraction Blocker to keep homework time Roblox-free.

FAQ

Blocking Roblox, answered

Can my child get around the block?
Not during a focus session. Distraction Blocker has no pause button, password or uninstall option while a session runs, and it blocks both the Roblox app and roblox.com in every browser. The only way out is a full PC restart, which is easy for a parent to notice.
Does it block Roblox on a phone, tablet or Xbox?
No. Distraction Blocker only works on the Windows PC it's installed on. For other devices, use Roblox's own parental controls (they apply to the account everywhere) or the device's built-in screen-time settings.
Can Roblox still be played after homework?
Yes. Roblox is only blocked during focus sessions. It unlocks automatically during breaks and when the session ends, so you decide when Roblox time is, not the app.
Do I need a Roblox parent account or a Microsoft account?
Not for Distraction Blocker. It works with no account at all. Roblox's built-in parental controls need a linked parent account, and Microsoft Family Safety needs your child to sign in to Windows with a Microsoft account in your family group.
Is Distraction Blocker really free?
Yes. There's no subscription, no trial and no paid tier. Every feature, including the locked session, is free.
